# 02 | Global Job Architecture: Job Families, Levels, Profiles and Careers

## Architecture design
**Job family -> subfamily -> global job profile -> career track/level -> local position/title -> employee assignment.** Store immutable job codes separately from local display titles.

| Job family | Example subfamilies (illustrative) |
| --- | --- |
| Ground Operations | Ramp Services, Station Operations, Cargo Handling |
| Customer Experience | Passenger Services, Service Quality |
| Commercial | Account Management, Sales Operations, Customer Solutions |
| Technology & Data | Enterprise Systems, Data Analytics, Cybersecurity |
| Corporate Services | HR, Finance, Procurement, Risk |

## Career tracks — original illustrative descriptions
| Track/level | Typical contribution | Decision scope | People leadership |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IC1 | Routine, supervised delivery | Established tasks | None |
| IC2 | Independent skilled execution | Within defined practice | None |
| IC3 | Senior specialist | Complex local problems | Peer coaching |
| IC4 | Principal/expert | Multi-team problems, standards | Functional influence |
| IC5 | Enterprise specialist | Enterprise-wide complexity | Cross-business influence |
| M1 | Team manager | Daily team performance | Direct team |
| M2 | Functional manager | Multi-team planning | Multiple teams |
| M3 | Director | Multi-site/function results | Managers of managers |
| M4 | Enterprise executive | Global business strategy | Leadership groups |

Levels are **not** direct pay grades. Regulated/operational certification, labor market and collective agreement constraints require local policy treatment. Not every career can progress equally through both tracks.

## Naming policy
- Job profile names describe stable **work content**, not incumbent seniority or honorific titles.
- A standardized profile can have approved local display titles; equivalence is documented, not assumed.
- Separate job family from org chart; the same job can sit in different business units.
- Promotion requires accountable scope assessment, not simply renaming or tenure.
- Every role has approved purpose, 5–8 core accountabilities, decision rights, interfaces, critical skills and experience prerequisites.

## Sample approved job profile template
**Global job code:** OPS-STATION-M1  
**Global title:** Station Operations Manager  
**Family:** Ground Operations / Station Operations  
**Track/level:** Manager / M1  
**Purpose:** Manage daily operational delivery, safety, people and service KPIs at an assigned station within approved authority.  
**Accountabilities:** workforce roster, incident response, vendor coordination, compliance, customer service, safety escalations, cost controls and performance review.  
**Role boundaries:** local safety certification and regulatory requirements remain binding; financial approval thresholds documented locally.  
**Skills:** safety/quality management, incident leadership, stakeholder management, workforce planning, HRIS literacy.  
**Interfaces:** airport operations, airline representatives, customer experience, HR, finance.  
**Approval route:** Operations owner -> Local HR -> Global OD -> Reward as required -> HRIS activation.

## Career movement
Document lateral pathways through shared competencies (e.g., Customer Experience -> Service Quality -> Operational Excellence), assessment gaps and training without promising promotion or grade equivalence.

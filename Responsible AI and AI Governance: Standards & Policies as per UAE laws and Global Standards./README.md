## Responsible AI Standards and Policies

To put governance into practice, organizations need clear standards and policies. For Dubai/UAE entities, responsible AI should align local AI ethics and governance guidance with global management and risk standards.

## 1. Dubai / UAE Perspective — first

In Dubai and the UAE, responsible AI is guided by local frameworks that set clear expectations for how AI should be developed and used. **This includes:**

- **Digital Dubai AI Ethics** — Dubai toolkit for responsible AI, focused on fairness, accountability, transparency and explainability.
- **AI Ethics Self-Assessment Tool** — Helps AI developers/operators assess ethical performance, gaps and mitigation measures for each AI system.
- **UAE AI Charter** — National principles for ethical AI, privacy and data security, human oversight, accountability and compliance.
- **Dubai Universal Blueprint / AI Seal** — Dubai roadmap and trust mechanism for AI adoption, AI leadership and verified AI suppliers.

**Legal-status clarification:** These four items are *not* all binding laws. The ethics guidelines and self-assessment are recommended guidance; the Charter states policy principles; the Blueprint sets strategic direction; the AI Seal concerns suppliers. Binding requirements must be separately assessed, including applicable data-protection law.

Full explanation: [01_UAE_Frameworks_and_Laws.md](01_UAE_Frameworks_and_Laws.md).

## 2. Global Standards and Policies — second

Local expectations are supported by global standards that provide practical ways to manage AI governance, risk and accountability. **Key standards include:**

- **ISO/IEC 42001:2023** — AI Management System requirements for policies, roles, risk treatment, monitoring and continual improvement.
- **ISO/IEC 23894, 42005, 38507** — Guidance for AI risk management, AI impact assessment and governance of AI use by organizations.
- **NIST AI RMF 1.0 + GenAI Profile** — A practical Govern, Map, Measure and Manage approach for trustworthy and generative AI risk.
- **EU AI Act, OECD and UNESCO** — Risk-based obligations and human-centered principles for safe, transparent and accountable AI.

**Legal-status clarification:** The EU AI Act is binding only where applicable. ISO, NIST, OECD and UNESCO documents have different voluntary/standards/recommendation status; citing them does not itself establish compliance.

Full explanation: [02_Global_Standards_and_Policies.md](02_Global_Standards_and_Policies.md).

### Method

Register AI system → determine governing UAE requirements → map international standards → identify ethical/security/privacy risks → design controls → define test protocol → review evidence → approve or hold → monitor and reassess.

### Evidence integrity

All processes, thresholds, results and roles are **proposed**, unless explicitly identified as sourced official guidance. There are **no real candidate records, customer data, executed AI tests, independent audits, certification claims or verified client outcomes** in this project.

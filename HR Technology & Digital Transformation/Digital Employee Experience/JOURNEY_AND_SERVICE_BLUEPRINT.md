# HR employee journeys and service blueprint

## Journey A — New starter (fictional)
| Stage | Employee task | Frontstage experience | Backstage coordination | Friction hypothesis | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Offer accepted | Confirm next steps | One plain-language checklist | Recruitment -> HR handoff | Duplicated document requests | Recruiter / new-joiner interviews |
| Before day one | Verify readiness | Timeline and help contact | HR, IT, facilities and hiring manager | Unclear owner for equipment | Task walkthrough |
| Day one | Gain access and orientation | Accessible welcome and support routes | Identity provisioning | Failure not explained | Service ticket sampling |
| Week one | Complete required steps | Task status and reminders | HR ops follow-up | Email-only chasing | Time-to-complete baseline |
| Month one | Seek policy answers | Human support + approved FAQ | Policy owner | Outdated language | Content validation |

## Journey B — Routine HR certificate request
Discover service -> check eligibility guidance -> select request type -> confirm non-sensitive details -> receive tracking ID -> HR processing -> status notification -> completed artifact / human follow-up.

## Service blueprint separation
- **Frontstage:** employee-facing wording, confirmation, service status, escalation.
- **Backstage:** HR decision maker, document controls, approvals, case assignment and service-level monitoring.
- **Supporting systems:** an approved ticketing tool and existing HRMS; these are solution options and not connected.
- **Failure path:** employee can contact a named HR team without depending on an automated assistant.

## Design principles
Plain language, mobile first, accessible form labels, visible ownership, choice of channel, data minimisation, transparent timelines, no dark patterns and no silent automated employment decisions.

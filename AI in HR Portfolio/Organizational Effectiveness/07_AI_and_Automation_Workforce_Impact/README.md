# 07 | AI & Automation Impact Assessment for Group Work Redesign

## Assessment at task, not job-title, level
Inventory work activities; service outcomes and criticality; volumes and time; exception handling; needed human judgement; legal/employee risks; technical feasibility; identity/data access; change-readiness and redeployment potential. Never assume a role disappears because some tasks are automatable.

## Controlled capacity hypothesis
Eligible hours × automation exposure × adoption factor × (1 − rework factor) = theoretical recoverable hours. In this synthetic case adoption 75%, rework 15%; the result is a **scenario**, not validated value realised.

Three model activities: employee document routing; workforce reporting preparation; grade/termination decisions. High-impact grade/termination decisions are **excluded** from autonomous capacity/savings estimates; human governance and any lawful consultation remain central.

## Review gates
1. HR/CIO service owners validate a real task map and baseline.
2. Legal/Privacy/InfoSec classify data, access and impact, including jurisdictions.
3. Reward/ER assess role and worker impacts; skills owners map redeployment.
4. AI validation uses documented precision, exception rate, bias/quality tests, auditability, fallback and failure modes.
5. Pilot runs under measurable service outcomes and human oversight.
6. Finance verifies *actual* time/cash outcomes once measured; avoid double-counting redeployment and cash reductions.

## Future human–AI control pattern
Human caseworker owns exception and sensitive outcomes; automation may draft, route or identify discrepancies; a named human role approves hires, grades, employee relations and terminations; log input source and version, approval, action and remediation. Never place identifiable employee data into unmanaged public prompts.

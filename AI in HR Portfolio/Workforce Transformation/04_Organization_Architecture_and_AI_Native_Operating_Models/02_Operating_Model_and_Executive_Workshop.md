# Operating-model design and executive workshop

## Target operating-model components
| Component | Accountable human | Responsible delivery contributors | Decision that cannot be delegated |
|---|---|---|---|
| HR policy guidance | HR policy owner | HR service + approved retrieval agent | Employee entitlement / adverse action |
| Recruitment coordination | TA director | Recruiting team + scheduling tools | Candidate selection / rejection |
| Customer service summaries | Customer operations head | Service SMEs + summarization agent | Regulated or disputed remedies |
| Workforce planning | CHRO with CFO | People analytics, finance, business heads | Hiring, restructuring, redeployment |
| AI platform / agent identity | CTO | Engineering, IT operations, MLOps | Security exception and production access |
| AI risk and incident response | CISO / accountable AI executive | Cyber, legal, privacy, product owner | Risk acceptance / suspend release |

A=one accountable party per decision; align subsidiary exceptions through documented escalation. Work may be dynamic; **accountability remains identifiable**.

## Example end-to-end exception
Employee asks, "Should this performance rating trigger termination?" -> AI classifies **high consequence** -> no auto-decision or HRIS write -> HR manager receives source/uncertainty summary only if lawful and authorized -> human evaluates with policy and due process -> audit event and appeal route -> independent assurance review.

## 90-minute CEO/CHRO/CTO design workshop
- Minutes 0–15: agree purpose, business metrics and excluded decisions.
- Minutes 15–30: decompose one value stream into tasks, exceptions and handoffs.
- Minutes 30–45: map human vs AI work and non-delegable approvals.
- Minutes 45–60: compare centralized/federated/outcome-pod structures.
- Minutes 60–75: resolve data owner, integration, budget, identity and skills dependencies.
- Minutes 75–90: choose pilot boundaries, sponsor, success measures, open risks and next gate.

## Decisions to record
- Stage 0: business case and service baseline.
- Stage 1: accountable business owner and privacy/security signoff.
- Stage 2: design approval, risk acceptance limits and apprenticeship safeguards.
- Stage 3: shadow evaluation, user feedback and *hold/go* criteria.
- Stage 4: measured pilot versus baseline; changes only through a human steering decision.

## Example change and learning design
Skills: problem framing, exception judgment, policy source checks, data literacy, agent monitoring and workflow design. Junior employees retain supervised case rotations, shadowing and coaching rather than losing all early-career learning activities.

## Prototype scoring model
The CSV and script compare **synthetic design options** by handoff burden, unresolved accountability, unmitigated risks and documented learning pathways. Automatic disqualification applies if a model has accountability gaps or unmitigated high-risk decisions; remaining scores do not imply a recommendation to implement. Governance votes cannot be replaced by a score.

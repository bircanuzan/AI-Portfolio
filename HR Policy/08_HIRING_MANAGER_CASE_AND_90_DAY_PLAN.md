# 08 | Hiring-Manager Case Study and First 90 Days

**Independent fictional scenario for a multinational airline; sector_organization role requirements used as an external benchmark only.**

## Leadership decision: approve a controlled HR policy modernization
### Challenge
Six-country operations, a fragmented template library, local-law changes and 24/7 aviation service requirements create inconsistent employee answers, contract risk, payroll exceptions and manager decision delays. The ambition is **not** one uniform employment rule across all jurisdictions: it is one accountable governance architecture with legally defensible local variants.

### Options
| Option | Advantages | Limitations | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Country-only ownership | High local responsiveness | Fragmented control and weak group audit | Reject as sole model |
| B. One rigid global policy | Fast theoretical standardization | Material legal/consultation/operational risks | Reject |
| C. Global principles + controlled country addenda | Standardized lifecycle and evidence, local legal authority and operations fit | Requires cross-functional coordination | **Recommend** |

### Approval requested (fictional)
Approve a Group HR Policy Office operating charter; select two local-counsel-cleared pilot markets after readiness review; ringfence **synthetic AED 65,000** for policy review, workflow/UAT and comms; require a 90-day risk/benefit report; do not launch changes in jurisdictions with unresolved legal, consultation, payroll or safety dependencies.

### Business-case narrative
Illustrative annual admin capacity value **AED 36,000** plus correction rework avoided **AED 12,000**, against **AED 65,000** one-off budget. Year-one net **AED −17,000**, with simple payback about **16.25 months** under the stated assumptions. Savings are **unverified projections**, not promised financial results. A legally necessary change must proceed regardless of ROI, with any economic analysis guiding implementation choices—not employee entitlements.

## First 30 days — discover, classify, protect
- Meet CHRO, Group/Country Legal, HRBPs, Employee Relations, Operations, Payroll, Reward, Finance, Data Privacy and HRIS.
- Inventory policies, contract/handbook variants, signing authority, translations, country addenda, payroll links and active exceptions.
- Build country employment-law question register with counsel-appointed verification owners, source dates and deadlines.
- Identify immediate hold/repair risks: outdated clauses, employee-facing contradictions, unapproved versions and statutory change dates.
- Capture baseline policy-ticket and payroll correction rates, employee feedback and user pain points.
- **Gate:** no global publication before valid local review.

## Days 31–60 — design and draft
- Agree policy hierarchy, document taxonomy, RACI, materiality tiers, review cycles, approval SLAs and audit evidence.
- Draft priority policies, local-addendum formats, contract clause inventory and employee handbook change summaries.
- Design HRIS/payroll impact matrix, UAT scripts, training and employee communications.
- Prepare Finance-validated cost assumptions and balanced success measures.
- Run controlled employee/manager comprehension testing with appropriate privacy safeguards.
- **Gate:** legally reviewed draft; signed operational control owners and pilot readiness criteria.

## Days 61–90 — pilot and assure
- Complete final legal, operational, payroll and HRIS approval for selected pilot countries.
- Apply effective-dated release, version locking, localized FAQ and manager/HRBP briefing.
- Run controlled acceptance tests, escalation/rollback rehearsals and support-desk monitoring.
- Review employee cases, appeals, anomalies, legal issues, defects and data-quality concerns.
- Present a 90-day sponsor dashboard with root-cause fixes, evidence of improvements and next-market sequencing.
- **Gate:** expansion only with validated local requirements and clean release evidence.

## Example difficult interview questions and sound responses
**Q: Global leadership wants a single annual-leave standard tomorrow. What do you do?**  
A: Establish intent and population; map mandatory local entitlements and collective/contract obligations with counsel; design common global principles only where possible; quantify cost and system impacts; release approved local schedules with effective-dated notices.

**Q: Payroll discovers that a policy change caused underpayments.**  
A: Escalate immediately with Legal, Payroll and sponsor; establish employee impact and lawful correction plan; pause further affected releases; reconcile individual cases securely; correct workflows, documentation and controls; monitor for recurrence.

**Q: Legal approves but Operations says it cannot roster safely.**  
A: Resolve the actual safety/operational constraint with both functions, explore lawful alternatives and exception criteria; never bypass mandatory employee rights or safety controls; escalate trade-offs to delegated authority with documentation.

**Q: Would you let generative AI draft country policy changes?**  
A: Use approved enterprise tooling only for assisted first drafting and redline analysis; insist on source attribution and version control; have qualified local counsel validate legal meaning, then control-owner and sponsor sign-off before publication.

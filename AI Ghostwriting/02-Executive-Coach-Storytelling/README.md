# Executive Coach: LinkedIn Storytelling & Voice Adaptation
**Independent speculative portfolio sample | Fictional persona, not a client engagement**

## Brief and audience
Create distinct content for an executive coach writing to newly promoted managers. This illustrates the founders/coaches/CEOs scope of multi-client ghostwriting. The voice is reflective, warm and direct, with questions rather than startup rhetoric.

## Voice rules
Use recognizable management situations. Preserve nuance. Do not invent coaching sessions, client quotations, qualifications, diagnoses or outcomes. Avoid implying that every difficult conversation can be solved by a script.

## Illustrative source note
A fictional coach's approved teaching premise: managers sometimes turn a request for advice into a request for permission by solving every problem themselves. This is a constructed exercise input, not a real interview.

## Post 1: The question before the answer
When someone asks, “What should I do?”, your first answer can wait.

Try asking:

“What options have you considered?”

The point is not to make help harder to get.

It is to understand what kind of help is needed.

Perhaps they have two reasonable options and need context you hold.
Perhaps they lack authority to make the decision.
Perhaps they are worried about being blamed if it goes wrong.

Those are different problems.

A quick instruction can solve today's task while leaving the underlying problem untouched.

A useful conversation makes the boundary clear:

Here is what you can decide.
Here is what needs approval.
Here is when I want you to come back.

Giving someone ownership takes more than saying, “You own this.”

It takes a shared understanding of what ownership allows them to do.

What decision could your team make without waiting for you this week?

## Post 2: Delegation with a boundary
“Use your judgment” can be a confusing instruction.

One person hears freedom.
Another hears a test they might fail.

Make the instruction more useful:

You can choose the approach.
The budget limit is fixed.
Bring me in if the deadline or customer commitment changes.
We will review the outcome on Friday.

Now there is room to think and a boundary to work within.

Clarity does not remove judgment.

It gives judgment somewhere to operate.

Before you decide that someone lacks confidence, check whether you have made the decision space clear.

## Alternative hooks
- Delegation fails quietly when the boundaries stay in your head.
- Your team may need a clearer decision, not another confidence talk.
- “Use your judgment” needs a second sentence.

## Adaptation notes
Compared with the founder sample, this voice uses fewer product terms and slower, more reflective pacing. It gives managers useful language without claiming a universal method or fabricated coaching success.

## Revision demonstration
Draft: “Empower your team to unlock their full leadership potential.”
Edited: “Tell people which decisions they can make without waiting for you.”
Reason: replaces an abstract promise with observable behavior.

## Review and measurement plan
Confirm fit with the coach's actual method. Check tone, confidentiality and whether the closing question invites a substantive response. Proposed measures: saves, relevant manager discussions and inquiries attributable to the post. No actual results are represented.

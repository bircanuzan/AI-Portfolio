## Synthetic interview excerpt
Interviewer: What do buyers miss when they evaluate a retrieval assistant?
Expert: They ask whether the answer sounds right. I also want to see which document version was used and what happens when two documents disagree.
Interviewer: What should the assistant do then?
Expert: Show the conflict and route the question to the right owner. It should not quietly blend the two policies.
Interviewer: Is that a product claim?
Expert: No. This is a design recommendation for the exercise.

## Final LinkedIn post
Two documents. Two different answers. One confident assistant.

That is a useful test case for a company knowledge tool.

Suppose an old policy allows an expense and a newer one requires approval.

An assistant might retrieve both.

A fluent answer can hide the disagreement by combining them into something neither document actually says.

A more useful response would make the uncertainty visible:

These documents conflict.
Here are their dates and owners.
This question needs confirmation before you act.

That is why I would review more than the final answer.

I would ask which version was retrieved, whether the sources agree and who owns unresolved questions.

A source link helps.

A source link to the wrong version does not settle the question.

If you are testing a knowledge assistant, add one pair of deliberately conflicting documents to the test set.

Then check what the system does with the conflict.

## Short version
An AI assistant can cite a document and still leave you with the wrong policy.

Check the version.
Check for conflicting guidance.
Check who owns the decision when sources disagree.

Those checks belong in the evaluation, alongside answer accuracy.

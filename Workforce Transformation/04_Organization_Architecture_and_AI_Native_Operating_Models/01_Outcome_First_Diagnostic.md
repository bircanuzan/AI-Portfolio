# Outcome-first organization diagnostic

**Fictional problem:** Atlas UAE Shared Services has separate HR service, recruitment operations, customer operations and finance-support teams. Automated summaries and policy assistance could change their workflows; executives disagree on who owns quality, data, agent cost and exceptions. There are **no measured volumes or actual client results**.

## Executive purpose-to-organization trace
| Layer | Questions and illustrative decision | Evidence required before acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Deliver reliable, fair, timely employee/customer support | CEO-approved strategy |
| Value | Faster *correct* resolution without privacy breaches | Cost, quality and experience baselines |
| Outcomes | Verified quality, first-time resolution, audited exceptions | KPI owners / measurement design |
| Work | Clarification, judgment, retrieval, drafting, approvals, escalation | Task observation and case taxonomy |
| Human + AI | AI drafts only low-risk answers; human handles personal or consequential cases | Evaluation, access boundaries, human approvals |
| Capabilities | Risk judgment, data literacy, coaching, service design, agent testing | Skills assessment and training |
| Organization | Named business value owner, service owners, platform owner and risk owners | Approved decision-rights matrix |

The ordering above is **informed by publicly discussed G42 outcome-first design principles**; this is an original synthetic application and is not presented as G42 internal work.

## Discovery interview cards
- **CEO / CFO:** Which measurable outcomes justify redesign? Where is capacity redeployed rather than assumed saved?
- **CHRO:** What employee decisions are non-delegable? How do junior professionals develop judgment if routine tasks change?
- **CAIO / CTO:** Which systems, identities, APIs and lifecycle controls will agents use? Who can revoke access?
- **CISO / Legal:** What is prohibited by data classification, privacy or employment requirements? Who signs risk acceptance?
- **Operations:** Which handoffs and exception types are most costly? What makes a case high consequence?
- **Employees:** Which work requires empathy, discretion, accessibility or apprenticeship? How is appeal handled?

## Three organizational options
1. **Centralized AI service / CoE:** strong standards and shared platform controls; possible domain bottlenecks.
2. **Federated functional ownership:** domain expertise and accountability; possible duplicated architecture and inconsistent standards.
3. **Outcome-based cross-functional pods:** operations, IT, product, risk and HR own an outcome together; needs explicit enterprise guardrails and scarce specialist capacity.

## Selection criteria (non-financial first)
- A human remains accountable for employee and customer decisions.
- Shared security and data standards apply regardless of model.
- Apprenticeship/early-career learning is preserved through supervised work.
- Exception handling is operationally funded.
- Success is defined as verified value, trust and decision quality, **not headcount reduction**.

# Benefits realization, investment gates and CFO scorecard

## Benefits lifecycle
1. **Sponsor hypothesis:** Define exact outcome, control owner and causal mechanism.
2. **Baseline:** Validate service demand, quality, rework, cost and comparison group.
3. **Proof of value:** Pilot with real approvals, monitoring and representative workload only if authorized.
4. **Measurement:** Quality, review time, human override, incidents, adoption, vendor spend and employee outcomes.
5. **Finance validation:** Distinguish capacity released, cost avoidance, revenue or service uplift, and realized cash savings.
6. **Executive gate:** Steering committee decides scale, pivot or stop; no automated employment decisions.
7. **Sustain:** Monthly business-benefits review and quarterly CHRO/CFO/CAIO report.

## Executive benefit register
| Metric | Definition | Evidence required | Accountable owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Correct first-time resolution | Cases meeting standard without reopening | Service logs and independent QA | Operations |
| Net human review burden | Human correction, approvals and exception minutes | Time study, case-level telemetry | Operations |
| Capacity released | Quality-adjusted human hours freed | Baseline and representative comparison | Workforce planning |
| User experience | Accessible satisfaction and trust | Surveys and segmented feedback | Product + CHRO |
| Skill mobility | Demonstrated capability changes | Assessed learning, validated skills | CHRO |
| AI unit operating cost | Total vendor, build, usage and support expense / accepted task | Invoices and operating data | CAIO/Finance |
| Risk and privacy | Incident rates and high-consequence failures | Independent test and incident evidence | CISO/Legal |
| Realized financial benefit | Documented cost, revenue or budget change | Finance reconciliation / ledger evidence | CFO |

## Worked illustration
A process may free 100 gross hours, consume 30 hours of reviews, and require additional AI and training expense. The resulting 70-hour potential capacity release **does not prove cash savings** or justify layoffs. If work volume is growing, improved service resilience might be the actual benefit.

## Investment options
- A: process simplification without AI
- B: bounded AI with human approval
- C: internal skills and capability redesign
- D: specialist external service / buy

Each requires risk, uncertainty, data/privacy and cost comparison. Do not double-count time benefits across several agents or departments.

## Decision rights
CFO owns monetary claims. CHRO owns employment and workforce impacts. CAIO/CTO owns technology readiness. Operations owns quality, service and work estimates. CISO/Legal own risk reviews. Steering group signs final go/no-go.

## Implementation maturity
The Python model uses invented CSV inputs and returns a *hypothetical* labor-time equivalent and net opportunity. It expressly outputs verified cash savings as zero and does not report actual ROI. Real benefit claims require a verified client baseline and independent review.

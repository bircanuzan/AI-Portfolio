# Client problem → method → decisions → controls

## Hypothetical employer scenario
A UAE-based multi-function employer asks its HR transformation team to identify *where to investigate employee departures* and propose decision-quality dashboards. This is **not a real employer engagement**.

## Workflow
1. Confirm metric glossary, period, headcount populations, and role-based data access.
2. Profile data and validate duplicate IDs, cohorts, allowed values and missing records.
3. Compute overall and departmental fixed-cohort attrition fractions with explicit denominators.
4. Distinguish group-level descriptions from hypotheses; inspect sample size and data-generating limitations.
5. Ask managers about staffing mix, labor demand, survey administration and work design; do not identify individuals.
6. Prepare a business review with validation questions and a proposed pilot, not automatic actions.

## Business questions
- Is the definition of an exit consistent with internal mobility and leaves of absence?
- Are metrics comparable across quarters and offices?
- Are manager support/engagement scores voluntary, reliable and ethically collected?
- Are counts sufficient to avoid exposing individuals, especially small groups?
- Which intervention would be tested and what outcome would count as a positive result?

## Controls and guardrails
No age, nationality, religion, health, salary or real worker identity. No individual probability scores, automated employment decisions, or causal interpretations. In live data: lawful purpose, minimum-access, retention windows, pseudonymisation, small-cell suppression, human approval and documented source lineage.

## Decision recommendation
**DISCOVERY / VALIDATION** — do not implement attrition interventions on fabricated evidence. For a real pilot, require approved data and a non-causal, aggregate report; only then propose interventions with suitable evaluation designs.
